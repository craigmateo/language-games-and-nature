# Language Games and Nature

**Language Games and Nature: A Corpus-Based Analysis of Ecological Discourse** is my doctoral dissertation, completed at **TU Bergakademie Freiberg** in Germany.

The dissertation develops a corpus-based approach to ecological discourse, bringing together **corpus linguistics**, **discourse analysis**, **intercultural communication**, and ideas drawn from **Ludwig Wittgenstein** and **Hannah Arendt**.

It examines three environmentally focused case studies:

* genetically modified (GM) seed
* the Dakota Access Pipeline
* extractive mining

For each case, a custom corpus was constructed and analyzed at a different level of communication. The dissertation ultimately proposes a multilevel framework for studying ecological discourse and uses the concept of **language games (*Sprachspiele*)** to account for the plurality of scientific, cultural, political, economic, and ethical ways in which people make sense of environmental issues.

## Abstract

The dissertation approaches environmental discourse from the perspective of intercultural communication research and addresses the challenge of integrating communication, culture, and the natural world into a coherent analytical framework.

Discourse analysis is combined with corpus linguistics in a multilevel framework applied to three ecologically themed topics. The analyses highlight the coexistence of different perspectives and worldviews within environmental debates, including scientific, cultural, religious, and socio-economic forms of reasoning.

Drawing on Wittgenstein's concept of language games and Arendt's idea of the public sphere, the dissertation argues that environmental discourse is often characterized by decontextualization: participants may speak from different conceptual and cultural frameworks without recognizing those differences. More meaningful deliberation therefore requires greater awareness of the plurality of language games through which the natural world is interpreted.
